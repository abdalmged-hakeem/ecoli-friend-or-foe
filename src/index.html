    II

    Session II

    The hearing

    One species, two sides. The defence calls its witnesses, then the prosecution.

    The hearing

    Hear both sides

    Star witnessE. coli K-12Lab strain since 1922. Too tame to survive outside the lab.

    “It makes my insulin.”

    In 1978 the human insulin gene was put into E. coli on a plasmid. Since 1982, insulin made this way has been a medicine.

    “It makes vitamin K2.”

    Gut E. coli produce vitamin K2 (menaquinone).

    “It guards my gut.”

    By taking the space and food first, harmless strains make it hard for harmful bacteria to settle.

    “It taught us genetics.”

    How DNA copies itself, how genes switch on and off, how bacteria swap genes: all first worked out in E. coli. Seven Nobel Prizes.

    WantedE. coli O157:H7Carries Shiga toxin. As few as 1–100 cells can make you ill.

    Exhibit A: Shiga toxin

    It damages cells lining the gut and blood vessels. In some children it leads to kidney failure (HUS).

    The line-up

    Swipe →

    Exhibit B: how it reaches you

    Cattle carry O157:H7 without getting ill. It reaches people through undercooked minced meat, raw milk, unwashed produce, dirty water and unwashed hands.

    Exhibit C: resisting treatment

    ESBL-producing and multidrug-resistant E. coli are a major global health problem: some of our most important antibiotics no longer work on them.
